Anti-overclaim checklist

Start with the job

Customer taxonomy, workloads, and buying criteria

“AI data center” is not one segment. The product requirement changes with workload scale, operating model, sovereignty, existing estate, procurement, and failure economics.

Hyperscale

Job: build enormous, repeatable capacity at fleet economics with automation-first operations.

Buying criteria: radix/density, watts per bit, predictable supply, telemetry/API control, failure blast radius, qualification effort, optics economics, and multivendor leverage.

PM implication: expose primitives and lifecycle predictability; avoid assuming turnkey operations.

Neocloud / GPU cloud

Job: bring GPU clusters online quickly and sell reliable accelerator capacity.

Buying criteria: time-to-revenue, collective performance, tenant isolation, orchestration integration, observable SLOs, support, financing, and expansion without redesign.

PM implication: package validated designs, rapid deployment, assurance, and capacity signals.

Sovereign cloud

Job: deliver competitive compute while meeting jurisdictional control and assurance requirements.

Buying criteria: data/metadata residency, operator control, trusted supply, audit, encryption, lifecycle autonomy, local support, and ecosystem choice.

PM implication: sovereignty is an operating model and evidence set—not a “secure” label.

Enterprise

Job: add private AI and modern applications without destabilizing business-critical estates.

Buying criteria: operational familiarity, phased migration, segmentation, shared services, support, automation guardrails, security integration, utilization, and budget predictability.

PM implication: coexistence and day-2 simplicity can matter more than peak benchmark speed.

Public sector

Job: modernize mission workloads under procurement, accreditation, budget, and continuity constraints.

Buying criteria: certifications, transparent provenance, approved channels, long support windows, accessibility, disaster recovery, security controls, and skills availability.

PM implication: documentation, lifecycle notice, partner readiness, and evidence are product features.

Training

Synchronous distributed training can make the slowest rank determine iteration time. Collective patterns such as all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter, and all-to-all create coordinated, high-bandwidth exchanges. Model/data/expert parallelism changes traffic locality and skew.

Design lens: useful delivered goodput and tail completion time—not link utilization alone.

Inference

Online inference weights time-to-first-token and inter-token latency; batch/offline inference weights throughput. Disaggregated serving, model parallelism, KV-cache movement, and request skew can produce different traffic from training.

Design lens: separate user SLOs from backend throughput; do not reuse a training profile blindly.

RAG and data pipelines

Retrieval-augmented generation adds embedding, vector/keyword retrieval, policy, reranking, and data service paths. It can be storage- and service-network sensitive even when accelerator collectives are modest.

Design lens: trace the full request and data path before prescribing a “backend fabric.”

Traffic signatures to name in the interview

Elephant flows

Long, high-volume transfers can collide under hash-based ECMP and occupy queues. Measure flow completion and path imbalance.

Incast

Many synchronized senders target one receiver, driving a short, steep queue. Tune/validate at the actual fan-in and RTT.

Collective synchronization

A small delay on one rank can gate the job. Look at p99 collective completion and useful job throughput, not averages.

Architecture math

Clos / leaf-spine fabric foundations

State workload and failure assumptions first; then show path count, capacity, oversubscription, and operational boundaries. A diagram without math is decoration.

Whiteboard formulas

Leaf downlink capacity = server-facing ports × server-link rate

Leaf uplink capacity = fabric-facing ports × fabric-link rate

Oversubscription = aggregate downlink capacity : aggregate uplink capacity

Ideal equal-cost choices between two leaves = common active spines

“1:1” at the leaf means equal downlink and uplink line rate; it does not guarantee application nonblocking behavior. NIC limits, protocol overhead, traffic skew, failed links, hash collisions, receiver bottlenecks, and higher-tier capacity still matter.

Worked sizing example · fictional

Eight leaves each attach 32 servers at 400G: 12.8 Tb/s down per leaf. Each leaf has eight 800G uplinks, one to each of eight spines: 6.4 Tb/s up per leaf, or 2:1 leaf oversubscription.

With all links healthy, two leaves have eight path choices. A spine failure removes one path and 12.5% of that leaf’s uplink capacity; effective oversubscription becomes about 2.29:1 (12.8 ÷ 5.6). A four-leaf-to-four-leaf bisection is bounded by 4 × 6.4 = 25.6 Tb/s in one direction, before protocol and traffic-distribution effects.

Interview move: recompute under one failure and identify the SLO impact, not only the healthy-state number.

Multipathing is a policy

Per-flow ECMP preserves ordering but can strand bandwidth when a few elephant flows collide. Flowlet, packet spraying, or adaptive routing can use paths more finely, but must account for packet reordering, endpoint tolerance, telemetry freshness, and oscillation.

Failure-domain prompts

Link, optic, leaf, spine, rack/power, controller, routing process, software release, configuration, shared management, and region. For each: detect, contain, converge, drain/retry, recover, and prove.

SLO hierarchy

Link bandwidth/latency → flow goodput and completion → collective p50/p99 → job step time and goodput → cluster utilization → customer time-to-result. Availability must state scope and measurement window.

Congestion is the product problem

Ethernet AI fabrics: RoCE, queues, and control loops

RoCEv2 carries RDMA over UDP/IP, but “lossless Ethernet” is not a single switch setting. Endpoint behavior, queue mapping, headroom, marking, pause scope, routing, telemetry, and operations form one control system.

Explain RoCEv2 in 30 seconds

RDMA lets an application move data directly between registered memory regions with reduced CPU and copy overhead. RoCEv2 encapsulates the RDMA transport over UDP/IP, making it routable. The network still needs bounded congestion and loss behavior appropriate to the NIC/transport; that may combine ECN-based congestion control, carefully scoped PFC, QoS isolation, adequate buffering/headroom, multipathing, and telemetry. S18

CFO version: “We are protecting expensive accelerator time. The goal is not zero dropped packets at any cost; it is predictable job completion without a pause or hotspot wasting the cluster.”

Control-loop mental model

  1. Classify/map: endpoints and switches agree which traffic receives which priority/queue.
  2. Detect: a queue crosses an ECN marking threshold before exhausting safe capacity.
  3. Signal: marked packets reach the receiver; transport feedback reaches the sender.
  4. Adapt: the sender reduces then cautiously increases rate (algorithm-dependent).
  5. Protect: PFC may pause only the affected priority when imminent loss cannot be absorbed.
  6. Observe: marking, pause duration, occupancy, drops, CNP/rate, and application performance confirm behavior.

Lossless versus lossy is a workload decision

PFC-assisted: can protect loss-sensitive queues but introduces pause propagation and operational risk. ECN/loss-tolerant: avoids fabric pause dependency but requires endpoints/transports that recover efficiently. Many designs use both selectively rather than treating the whole fabric as lossless.

Congestion isolation

Bound the effect by tenant/workload/traffic class where hardware and scale allow; isolate compute from storage/control; use admission/capacity policy; detect hotspots; and make one receiver unable to pause unrelated work. Queue count is a scarce resource—budget it explicitly.

Load balancing and reordering

Hash-based ECMP is stable but coarse. Adaptive routing may respond to congestion; packet spraying uses paths more completely but reorders. Validate NIC reordering behavior, flow/flowlet boundaries, failure transitions, telemetry delay, and adversarial traffic—not only uniform benchmarks.

Overlay with evidence

Enterprise DC: BGP EVPN / VXLAN and operations

Separate underlay reachability, overlay control, tenant forwarding, and operational automation. That makes migrations and failure diagnosis tractable. S38 S39

Underlay

Routed leaf-spine connectivity provides resilient IP reachability between VTEP loopbacks. Common designs use eBGP, OSPF, or IS-IS according to scale and operations. Keep the underlay simple, observable, and independent from tenant route semantics.

EVPN control plane

MP-BGP distributes endpoint and reachability information. Route Type 2 advertises MAC/IP and Type 3 supports inclusive multicast Ethernet tag signaling; the EVPN IP Prefix route (Type 5) advertises IP prefixes. Exact use depends on design and implementation. S24 S25 S35

VXLAN data plane

VTEPs encapsulate tenant frames/packets in VXLAN over UDP/IP. VNIs identify overlay segments; L2VNI and L3VNI constructs support bridged and routed tenant services. The outer IP fabric can ECMP independently. S26

Multitenancy and anycast gateway

VRFs create tenant routing domains; VNIs bind overlay segments; route targets control import/export; policy determines permitted communication. A distributed anycast gateway presents a consistent gateway IP/MAC at participating leaves so east-west routing stays local. This improves path efficiency but raises consistency, duplicate-address, mobility, and operational-state requirements.

Security across control, management, and data planes

Start with assets, trust boundaries, adversaries, compliance, and recovery. “Encrypted” and “segmented” are properties to prove, not complete strategies.

Control plane

  • Restrict routing adjacencies and permitted peers/interfaces.
  • Authenticate protocols where supported and manage key rotation.
  • Use control-plane policing to protect CPU while preserving required protocols.
  • Filter route distribution; set maximums; monitor churn, leaks, and unexpected origins.
  • Test convergence and protection under malformed/high-rate traffic.

Management plane

  • Independent OOB path; least-privilege RBAC; MFA through approved identity design.
  • Secure management protocols, certificates, secrets, logging, time, backups, and break-glass.
  • Signed/verified software, vulnerability response, configuration provenance, and drift control.
  • Rate-limit/segment telemetry and automation collectors; protect their credentials and availability.

Data plane

Pipeline → bookings → revenue → adoption

Pipeline: potential value at defined qualification/stages. Bookings: accepted contractual order under company policy. Revenue: recognized under accounting rules. ARR: recurring annualized value under a defined convention. Adoption: activated and valuable use. Product PM inspects all of them: segment source, technical win, objections, stage aging, conversion, ASP/discount/mix, attach, deployment, usage, support, renewal/expansion, and losses.
